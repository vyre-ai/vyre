How it works
1Option-Return: hold it to talk while it is down; tap it to talk until the next tap. Final words submit as ⏎ would: a question answers at once, a follow-up continues the thread, "do ..." starts computer use.
2Listening: the level meter stands in for the mark, 5 bars, 2 wide, text-2, neutral. The footer says "⌥⏎ Release to ask" (hold) or "⌥⏎ Stop" (tap) and "Esc Cancel". The meter is static under Reduce Motion.
3Spoken replies: with "Read answers aloud" on, the answer to a spoken question is read aloud and the card's header shows "Speaking" with a small speaker icon. Esc, or a new question, stops it.
4First use is a row, not a dialog: "Voice needs a speech key", ⏎ copies vyre voice key deepgram; "Vyre needs the microphone to hear you", and macOS asks only after ⏎.
5"do " at the start, or ⌘⏎ on words that are not a question, starts an agent with hands and screen. The field keeps the words. First time: one row each for Accessibility and Screen Recording, ⏎ opens the right System Settings pane.
6The strip: 36 tall, right under the input, fill hover. The running ring, "Using your Mac", the agent, "Esc Stop" at the right. It announces once: "Vyre is using your Mac. Escape to stop."
7Steps: tool rows 28 tall, 13/18 text-2, the verb in words and the target in mono where it is a name. Done steps get a tick, the running step the ring. Steps grow, then scroll, like an answer.
8Holds: a send, post, payment or delete goes to the waiting list as an ask, with Touch ID in the panel. The agent waits, and its step reads "Waiting for you: Send to dana@harlowlegal.com".
9The menu bar mark: a 1.5 px ring in focus while an agent acts, and the tooltip "Vyre is using your Mac · Esc to stop", even with the panel hidden.
10Esc stops it: hands.stop and threads.interrupt in the same frame, then "Stopped. 3 steps done." for 4 s. Esc never needs Touch ID. When it finishes, the last row says what happened and ⌘O opens the session.
