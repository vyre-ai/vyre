Vyre 0.6.2 gives your work a shape. Records get views, pipelines and history, tasks get reviewers and approvers, the Flow editor shows how a Flow runs, people can ask to join a Space by its address, and a server serves the name you gave it.

## What's new

### Records

- Views for what is yours: Mine, Team and Everyone. A filter can say "me", and notes, calls, meetings and events carry their owner, so each view finds what belongs to you.
- The Records screen follows the approved layout: type tabs with counts, a row of views, and a "Say what you want to see" bar that turns a sentence into filters and says what it understood. Add a column from the table, group by a column, and see board column counts and totals for every record, not only the cards loaded.
- A record page opens with its name, its role pills, and quick actions: Log a call, Log a meeting, Add a note and New task. A record with an address has Send email, which opens the compose page with it filled in; nothing leaves from the record page.
- Pipelines for any type: a plain list of stages, fields a stage needs, a reason required for a lost one, several pipelines on one type, and a chance of closing on each stage. Settings > Objects edits them.
- Board totals: the count and sums for each stage or choice option of a type in one call, empty options included.
- Roles on a record (Lead, Client, Partner, Vendor, Teammate, Customer), each with its own stage and colour, so one person can be a Lead and a Client at once. A person or company page shows them as pills whose menu moves the stage.
- Link fields with an inverse (link one type to another and see it from both sides), relationships with a kind and dates, and roll-ups: a count, sum, average, minimum or maximum over the records that point at a record.
- Layouts: how a type is shown to you (columns, sort, group, filters, related lists), your own choices laid over the Space's, and a Space default an owner or admin can save.
- History: a record's changes with who made each (a person, or the assistant acting for them), the calls, meetings and notes logged on it, and an undo for a change that nothing later touched.
- Private notes and events: a note is read by its writer alone until shared with the record it is about or with the Space, and an outside-calendar event is read by its owner alone until shared. Everyone else sees Busy blocks (the time and nothing else). An owner or an admin does not read a member's private note either.
- Sample data: one call fills a Space with a small made-up studio (companies, people, deals, a project and tasks), and one call takes away exactly those and nothing you made.
- A new Space starts with People, Companies, Deals (a Sales pipeline: Lead, Qualified, Proposal, Won, Lost), Projects, Tasks, Events, Notes, Communications and Relationships. Files, Templates, Playbooks, Skills and team members no longer show as Records tabs.
- Fast fields: add a field and use it at once, and promote it to a real field later. Records also has a My tasks tab in three groups: Assigned to you, Reviewing and Approving.

### Tasks

- A task has three slots: Assigned, Reviewer and Approver. Reviewer and Approver take a person, an agent or a role. A task can also be assigned to a group, and the first member to start it takes it (a Claim it button on the task page).
- Tasks have new states: In review, Changes requested and Declined. Handing in goes to In review when there is a reviewer, who passes it on or asks for changes with a note. Nobody reviews their own work. In a Space where only the person who made the task could review it, the step is skipped and recorded ("No one else to check this").
- An approval after a review is signed over the review as well (its result, checklist and note), so an approver cannot be shown one review and sign another. The approver's role can require everyone to say yes.
- A late Reviewer or Approver can pass to a role the author named. Reviewer and Approver slots take a time limit, a fallback role and instructions and Space skills, in a Flow step or a Playbook to-do.
- An agent in the Reviewer slot, or the Approver slot for an internal check, is woken once for each hand-in. A doer agent is woken again when changes are requested, up to five rounds, then a person is given the task. A task's details reach a woken agent as information in a marked section, not as instructions. An agent can approve internal work only; a send or a stage hold always waits for a person.
- A task page shows where it stands: the strip of states, who is Assigned, Reviewer and Approver, and what the review said.

### Flows

- The Flow editor: several triggers on one Flow (each with its own condition), If and parallel steps shown as lanes side by side, a step can be turned off (skipped and recorded as off), and a docked panel beside the steps on a wide screen with Move up, Move down, Duplicate and Remove. Fields hold values as pills; a value can carry a fallback, {path | fallback}.
- Test this step tries one step against the Flow's last run and shows what went in, what would come out and what it would send, and says that nothing was changed or sent. It shows to an owner or an admin.
- Retry from this step runs a step and the ones after it again, and is off on steps at or before one that already sent something outward. The run history has All, Failed and Waiting filters and a search, and a step's input and output can be shown raw.
- A Kit's assistants are made when the Kit is installed and removed when it is removed. The install and update card quotes each assistant's instructions in full, marked as text from outside, so you read them before you approve.
- A Watchers tab in Flows lists every watcher with its state and its next and last run, and has Run now, Pause or Resume, Turn on and Delete. New watcher starts one from a source (mail, a calendar, a repo, Slack, a feed or a connection), tries it with Test, and turns it on.
- A "A webhook is called" trigger: a verified delivery starts the Flow at hook-<route> and its body reaches the Flow as trigger.<field>, as untrusted data, so the run is marked as started from outside. The route's signing secret is made in your Vault when you do not name one, and its value is never shown in a reply. A route that cannot be reached yet says why and has Check again.
- Playbooks can be edited on the page: add, rename, move and remove stages and to-dos in place, with an Assigned picker beside Reviewer and Approver on each to-do.

### People and Spaces

- Ask to join by address: a person enters a Space's address and asks to join. The owner turns on "Let people ask to join" (off by default) and sees each ask with the asker's four words and their note, then approves or declines. The person lands in the Space once approved.
- Settings > People and roles lists the people in the Space with their role (open one to change it, including to a role of your own), has an Invite someone button that makes a link with Copy, and lists the invitations waiting to be used with Revoke.
- A team member can open the timeline of a signature request they sent, download its audit file and list the requests waiting. They see only their own requests.
- A team member can upload a skill file as a draft of their own, which only they approve and use. A joined member also reaches notes, history, totals, Busy blocks and roles of Records, and can log a call, a meeting or a note, through the team's door.
- A team Space on a server is shown by its own name, not as "<name>'s Cloud". The sidebar's foot is a profile row with your face and a short menu, and Settings > Account has Change your look (sixteen colours). Apps is a grid of tiles. Objects pages have calm field rows and a link back to the list. Your look is kept on this device for now.
- Settings > What agents may do: each agent is shown by its name, every row opens its own page, and a tool is turned off by a search in plain words ("send an email") with "All of Records and contacts" for a whole area; the raw ids are behind Advanced.
- Chats opens a new thread with your Assistant.
- If your only AI account is an OpenRouter key, your Assistant now runs on it, starting with Claude Sonnet 4.5 through OpenRouter; change the model in Settings.
- Settings > Account shows your name, your devices and your recovery code, on the Mac app and on the web.

### Plugins and Vault

- A plugin's MCP server can ask for keys by name. Each missing key is a card in Needs you ("<plugin> needs a key: <VAR>"), and a key from the Vault reaches the server only for the calls it is granted for, to that one server and variable. Revoking a grant, deleting the item or a member leaving stops the server at once.

### Computers and Files

- Files: New folder makes an empty folder in the Space's Drive, and a project's folder shows the project's name.
- Files: Move to... opens a folder picker, and a file, a folder or a selection can be dragged onto a folder to move it there, with Undo.
- Chrome's downloads in an agent's computer land in a folder the browser's user owns and the agent can read but not change; Files shows it read-only as Downloads.

## Fixed

- A check given to a role (a Reviewer or Approver that is a role) now finds the Space members who hold that role. Before, every role resolved to nobody on a real server.
- Handing in a task with a file, a draft or fields now completes on a real server. The record it names must be one the person handing in can read, and a task's own record counts when the person who made the task could read it. A file in Drive is checked by its path.
- In the Computer tab, an agent's wake, watch, terminal and run-command tools work with per-agent users on: every wake used to fail for want of declared events, and only five of the computer's agent actions were wired.
- A new role could not be made on a real server: the New role dialog started with switches the box refuses for a Member and read the Space's record types by a field the box does not send. It now starts with what a Member has and reads the types by name.
- First run on a page with no daemon (the Mac window with no vyred, the hosted web app) made a device with no key-agreement point, so chats could not be wrapped to it ("This device isn't ready for private chats yet") and the box listed no device. New identities carry the point.
- A server whose Space was named never served that name, and the directory refused its certificate and address ("that name is not served by this server"). Naming now tells the directory that the server serves the Space, and the server takes the name (`vyre name status` and the public gate see it).
- `vyre name status` says what the public gate says: serving, waiting (the gate is up, the name does not point at it yet), getting a certificate, or failed with the reason. It used to stop at "named".
- A server install publishes its name and answers on port 443 as well as 7443, and a visitor at the bare name goes to the hosted app instead of a plain 404.
- Setting up an act that needs your yes no longer waits for a phone that is not paired: it says there is no way to approve it yet and what to do. In the Mac app every link now opens (it did nothing before), and the Claude sign-in has a field for the code the provider's page shows.
- A module whose start fails is stopped, so what it opened before it failed is closed. An edit made inside the record store is reported once, not twice. The ask-to-join Approve made an invite the directory could not verify; it now uses the Space's address label.

## Security

Security: in 0.6.1 the service that makes each agent's own Linux user on the Space computer (agentd) could not start, and agents used one shared browser with a separate browser context each. 0.6.2 starts agentd correctly. Per-agent users are still off by default (each agent has its own desktop computer) until the owner sets computers.agentUsers to true. With it on, if agentd is not ready, an agent's browser call waits up to 90 seconds (computers.agentdWaitMs) and is then refused with a plain message; it never falls back to the shared browser. The shared browser is used only when the owner sets computers.browse to "browser".
- A verified webhook delivery starts a Flow as an outside call, and only in the Space the route was opened for: its body is plain data, capped, and the run is marked untrusted. A route opened before this release has no recorded Space and starts no Flow until it is closed and opened again. The route's signing secret is made in your Vault when you do not name one, and its value is never in a reply.
- When the Space's owner has no presence device enrolled (no phone, no passkey), the owner's own approval steps pass on their signed-in session or on their own paired device. It never applies to an agent, a model, a module, a guest, a member or another person's session. Commands typed in a terminal (the vyre command) do not get this pass. With a device enrolled, the proof is asked as before. Settings > Privacy shows "Approvals: none needed until you pair a phone" while none is enrolled. Installing an app from this build's catalog on your own server, and removing one while keeping its data, ask for no proof; deleting an app's data still asks.
- Handing a Space over or making another person an owner still asks for a proof; with no phone or passkey paired, pair one first.
- The no-device pass ends when a phone with a secure key, a passkey or a Lumen key is enrolled. A browser's own key, and a key held in software (for example the ad-hoc Mac app's, or a browser paired earlier), does not turn approvals on.
- Ask to join is off for every Space until its owner turns it on at their own device. The door a person with no invite uses can call only requests.ask and requests.status. A forged ask cannot use up a Space's hourly allowance. An ask to a Space that is off, full, one the person is already in, or unknown gets the same words, and the note an asker writes is plain text that no model reads.
- An uploaded team skill is read strictly and kept as a draft only its owner approves and uses; a plugin shape, a non-text file, a credential or an oversized zip refuses the whole upload.
- A plugin's key grant is shown with what the kernel knows first (plugin, level, who it is for, item, variable) and the plugin author's command line last as a quoted, cut list, so an argument cannot write the sentence; a key for a server is granted to the code it runs, so a new version with a different command, different arguments or different needed keys waits to be granted again. A private note is not read by an owner or an admin either.

## If you installed 0.6.1

Update the app and your server to 0.6.2, then open the app and unlock it with your password. A few things repair themselves on that unlock or after the server starts:

- Private chats. A device made on a page with no daemon (the Mac window with no vyred, the hosted web app) had no key for private chats. The unlock adds it once, and the device then shows in the device list.
- Your server's name. If you named a Space on a server and it never answered at its name, the unlock tells the names directory that the server serves it, once for each Space you own on a paired server. The server takes the name by itself a few seconds after it starts on 0.6.2. Then `vyre name status` shows where it stands: serving, waiting, getting a certificate or failed with the reason.

Nothing here asks you for anything but your password, and each repair runs once.

## Known gaps

- Per-agent Linux users and the shared browser on the Space computer are opt-in (computers.agentUsers and computers.browse), not the default.
- An agent's Chrome in the Space computer runs without Chrome's own renderer sandbox (Docker's default profile refuses the namespaces it needs), and without an address-space cap (a cap on committed memory holds it instead). It still reaches only that agent's own profile. A seccomp profile that allows it is planned for 0.7.
- A file or draft hand-in is checked by its form (a path or a record address that is there), not by looking at the file. A task with no checker takes the doer's word, so give any task that matters a checker.
- A skill that is edited and put back exactly during a task's review can pass unseen. Keep the list of people who may edit Space skills short.
- When a role must all say yes, a yes is kept as given. If a signing key is revoked after a yes and before the last, the earlier yes still counts. Reject the task so the round starts again.
- A group task whose taker leaves the group while the work is in review stays where it is until it moves. Ask the reviewer or approver to send it back.
- A skill a member uploads is untrusted text that only they approve and use. The upload counters (ten an hour, thirty a day) start again when the server restarts.
- Some Records features do not reach a person who joined a team yet: undo in history, putting a role on a record, sharing a synced event, and setup edits (a type's colour, a new field or link, sample data). Do them on the team's server, or as its owner. Planned for 0.6.3.
- A flood of forged asks to join can delay real ones for a minute at a time: the directory lookups behind the ask door are capped for the whole server (fifty a minute), so while a flood lasts a real ask is refused as rate limited and works in a later minute. It exposes nothing. Caps by network address are the relay's job.
- Until a phone with a secure key, a passkey or a Lumen key is enrolled, your own paired browser and Mac app act as you, so keep that browser to pages you trust.
- A Mac server's first key does not yet turn approvals on; that comes with signed builds.
- With no phone or passkey paired, a browser cannot pair new devices; pair your first phone (one with a secure key) from the Vyre app on your computer.
- The Mac app is ad-hoc signed until Apple signing, so it has no Touch ID approvals. An approval needs a paired phone with a secure key, or none is needed while no phone is paired. GitHub sign-in in the Mac app may not finish: its return page opens in the browser, not the app window.
- On the Mac app (ad-hoc signed), handing a Space over or adding an owner needs a paired phone with a secure key until Apple signing.
